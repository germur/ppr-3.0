{/*  Introduction  */}
<p class="text-[#111418] text-lg font-normal leading-relaxed mb-8">
                    Achieving a net-zero home in Florida isn't just about slapping solar panels on the roof; it requires a holistic approach starting with the building envelope and aggressive moisture control. With utility rates rising and hurricane intensity increasing, the dual goal of energy independence and physical resilience has never been more relevant for Florida homeowners.
                </p>

<span class="flex items-center justify-center size-8 rounded-full bg-[#1269e2] text-white text-lg font-bold shrink-0">1</span>
                        What 'Net-Zero Home Florida' Really Means
                    </h2>
<p class="text-gray-700 text-lg leading-relaxed mb-6">
                        In the Sunshine State, "Net-Zero" means battling the sun's heat while harvesting its energy. It’s a balance sheet equation: Energy Produced - Energy Consumed = 0. However, without a highly efficient shell, you’ll need a massive, expensive solar array to offset inefficient AC running 24/7.
                    </p>

<span class="flex items-center justify-center size-8 rounded-full bg-[#1269e2] text-white text-lg font-bold shrink-0">2</span>
                        Step One: Fix Moisture, Water Damage, and Mold
                    </h2>
<p class="text-gray-700 text-lg leading-relaxed mb-6">
                        Before sealing your home for efficiency, you must ensure it is dry. A tight home with moisture issues is a recipe for toxic mold. In Florida, humidity infiltration is the enemy.
                    </p>
<div class="bg-orange-50 border-l-4 border-secondary p-5 rounded-r-lg mb-8">

<p class="text-gray-700">
                            If you have existing leaks or past water damage, address them immediately. See our guide on <a class="text-secondary font-bold hover:underline" href="#">Identifying Hidden Water Damage in Stucco Walls</a> before proceeding with insulation upgrades.

<p class="text-gray-700 text-lg leading-relaxed mb-6">

<h2 class="text-[#111418] text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold leading-tight mb-6 flex items-center gap-3">
<span class="flex items-center justify-center size-8 rounded-full bg-[#1269e2] text-white text-lg font-bold shrink-0">4</span>
                        Right-Sizing HVAC, Hot Water, and Appliances
                    </h2>
<p class="text-gray-700 text-lg leading-relaxed mb-6">
                        Once the envelope is tight, you need less power to cool it. "Right-sizing" means installing smaller, more efficient variable-speed AC units that run longer at lower power to dehumidify effectively, rather than short-cycling blasts of cold air.
                    </p>

                        Solar, Batteries, and Real-World Florida Examples

<p class="text-gray-700 text-lg leading-relaxed mb-6">

                    </p>
